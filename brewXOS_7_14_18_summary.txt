[31][Normal] 7.15 AGE and Preparation of LB Liquid Medium and High-Pressure Steam Sterilization
[32][Normal] -AGE
[33][Normal] -Experimental Instruments：
[34][Normal] Electrophoresis line, electrophoresis tank, electrophoresis comb, gel-making tank, electrophoresis power supply (voltage regulator), gel exposure instrument
[36][Normal] -Experimental Principle:
[37][Normal] The movement of charged substances in an electric field is called electrophoresis. Gel electrophoresis using agarose gel is a commonly used method for separating and identifying DNA and RNA. This electrophoresis method uses agarose gel as the support and utilizes the charge effect and molecular sieving effect of DNA molecules during their movement to achieve the purpose of separating mixtures. DNA molecules carry a negative charge in solutions above their isoelectric point and move towards the anode in an electric field. Under a certain electric field strength, the migration speed of DNA molecules depends on the molecular sieving effect, that is, the size and configuration of the molecule are the main influencing factors. The migration speed of DNA molecules is inversely proportional to their relative molecular weight. Nucleic acid dyes are embedded in the double-stranded base pairs of DNA and emit purple light under ultraviolet irradiation.
[39][Normal] -Experimental steps:
[40][Normal] 1. Preparation of agarose gel: Weigh 2.5g of agarose (1% w/v), add 25ml (1x) electrophoresis buffer (TAE), shake evenly, and place it in a microwave oven to melt uniformly.
[41][Normal] 2. Preparation of gel plate: When the solution cools down to around 50 degrees Celsius, add 2.5ul of Ultra Blue (an alternative to EB), shake well, and pour it onto the gel plate. Remove air bubbles, let the gel cool and solidify, then gently lift the comb vertically.
[42][Normal] 3. Loading samples: Use a pipette to add the samples into the sample wells of the gel plate separately. After adding each sample, change the loading head to prevent contamination.
[43][Normal] 4. Electrophoresis: After loading the samples, connect the gel plate to the power supply for electrophoresis. During this process, the speed of DNA migration is proportional to the voltage. The samples move from the negative electrode to the positive electrode. When bromophenol blue moves about 1cm from the bottom edge of the gel plate, stop the electrophoresis.
[44][Normal] 5. Observation and recording: Remove the gel. Observe the staining under a UV lamp with a wavelength of 254nm. The DNA presence shows visible fluorescent bands. Take pictures and save them in the gel imaging system.

[50][Normal] -Experimental Conclusion:
[51][Normal] This experiment successfully separated and detected DNA fragments through agarose gel electrophoresis, confirming the expected size of the PCR products. The electrophoresis process is an important method for DNA analysis in molecular biology.

[122][Normal] 7.17 Construction and Transformation of Recombinant Strains  
[124][Normal] -Experimental Objective
[125][Normal] To introduce exogenous naked DNA directly into cells for replication, proliferation, and expression.  
[127][Normal] -Experimental Principle
[128][Normal] In a low-osmolarity calcium chloride solution at 0°C, bacterial cells swell into spherical shapes, losing some membrane proteins, which increases cell membrane permeability. Calcium ions and plasmid DNA form a hydroxyl-calcium phosphate complex that is resistant to degradation by DNase. This complex readily adheres to the bacterial cell surface, enhancing the likelihood of cellular uptake.  
[130][Normal] -Equipment and Materials
[131][Normal] Equipment: Micropipette, pipette tips, 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes, benchtop centrifuge, water bath, culture medium, spreader.  
[132][Normal] Materials: Gibson assembly ligation product from the previous experiment.  
[134][Normal] -Reagents
[135][Normal] Antibiotic-free sterile medium (2YT or LB).  
[137][Normal] -Experimental Procedure
[138][Normal] 1. Thawing Competent Cells: Remove BL21(DE3) competent cells from -80°C and immediately place them on ice. After 5 minutes, once the cell pellet has thawed, add the target plasmid and mix gently by flicking the bottom of the EP tube (avoid pipetting up and down). Keep on ice for 25 minutes.  
[139][Normal] 2. Heat Shock:Transfer the tube to a 42°C water bath for 45 seconds, then immediately return it to ice and incubate for 2 minutes. Avoid agitation, as it may reduce transformation efficiency.  
[140][Normal] 3. Recovery: Add 700 μL of antibiotic-free sterile medium (2YT or LB) to the tube, mix well, and incubate at 37°C with shaking at 200 rpm for 60 minutes.  
[141][Normal] 4. Centrifugation and Plating: Centrifuge at 5000 rpm for 1 minute to pellet the cells. Discard most of the supernatant, leaving ~100 μL to gently resuspend the pellet. Spread the suspension onto 2YT or LB medium containing the appropriate antibiotic.  
[145][Normal] 5. Incubation:Invert the plate and incubate overnight at 37°C.  
[147][Normal] -Experimntal Precautions
[148][Normal] The durations of heat shock and ice incubation must be precisely controlled, with rapid transition between the two steps.  
[149][Normal] Avoid excessive force when flicking the EP tube, as it may cause bacterial cell death.  
[151][Normal] -Experimental Results
[152][Normal] Precipitate formation was observed in the centrifuged bacterial culture.
